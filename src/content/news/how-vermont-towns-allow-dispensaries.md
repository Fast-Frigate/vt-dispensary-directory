title: "Why some Vermont towns have dispensaries and others never will"
description: "Vermont requires towns to opt in before a retail cannabis establishment can open. That single rule explains most of the map."
publishDate: 2026-08-24
---

Look at where Vermont's licensed dispensaries actually are and the distribution seems strange. Burlington has more than twenty. Large stretches of the state have none at all, including towns big enough that you would expect at least one.

The explanation is a single provision in Vermont law: local opt-in.

## How opt-in works

A cannabis retailer cannot open in a Vermont town unless that town has affirmatively voted to permit cannabis retail establishments. Not zoned for it. Not declined to ban it. Voted yes.

That vote typically happens at Town Meeting or on a local ballot. Towns that have never held the vote are closed to retail by default, and a licence from the Cannabis Control Board does not override it.

## Why this produces the map we have

The result is a patchwork rather than a distribution. Towns that moved early have clusters of shops competing with each other. Neighbouring towns that never took up the question have none, sometimes for miles.

It also means the map is not fixed. Towns can and do hold the vote later. A town with no dispensary today is not permanently closed; it is a town that has not voted yet, or has voted no once.

## What this means if you are searching

If your town shows nothing in this directory, the nearest shop may be one town over rather than genuinely far away. Browsing by county is often more useful than browsing by town for exactly this reason.

It also means a dispensary's presence tells you something about local politics, not about demand. Plenty of towns without a shop have residents driving twenty minutes to reach one.
