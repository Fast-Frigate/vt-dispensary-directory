description: "What to bring, what you can legally buy, what it costs, and what actually happens when you walk into a Vermont dispensary."

The first visit is the one people overthink. Here is what actually happens.

## Bring a valid ID

You need government-issued photo ID proving you are 21 or older. A passport, a driver's licence, a state ID card. Every licensed dispensary checks at the door, every visit, regardless of how old you look. No ID, no entry.

Medical patients registered with Vermont's program carry a separate registry card and shop under different rules, including access to some products adult-use customers cannot buy.

## What you can buy and carry

Vermont caps adult-use possession at one ounce of flower, or five grams of hashish. That is a possession limit, not a per-transaction limit, but in practice dispensaries will not sell you past it in one go.

The state also caps potency, which surprises people arriving from Massachusetts or New York. Flower is limited to 30% THC. Solid concentrates are limited to 60%. If you are used to seeing 80%-plus concentrates on a menu elsewhere, you will not find them here.

## What it costs

Expect roughly 20% in tax on top of the shelf price: a 14% cannabis excise tax plus Vermont's 6% sales tax. Menu prices at most shops are listed pre-tax, so the number at the register is higher than the number on the shelf.

Many dispensaries are cash-preferred or cash-only, a consequence of federal banking rules rather than local preference. Roughly half the shops in our directory accept debit. Check the listing before you drive.

## Where you cannot consume it

Public consumption is illegal in Vermont. That includes streets, parks, and vehicles. Consumption is for private property, and if you are renting, your landlord may have their own rules.

You also cannot take it across state lines. Cannabis remains federally illegal, and every border out of Vermont is a state line.

## A note on strain names

Strain names are marketing more than specification. Two jars labelled the same thing from two growers can test very differently. The certificate of analysis, which every licensed product has, tells you more than the name does. Ask to see it.
